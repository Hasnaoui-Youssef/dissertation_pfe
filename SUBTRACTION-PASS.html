02 The one decision I cannot make for you

Roughly twelve sentences across chapters 2 to 5 do nothing but name a figure or a table. "Figure 4.7 states the model." "Figure 4.10 states the components." "Table 3.7 states the environment in which the work was carried out." By the rule you gave me, every one of them is figure restatement and should go.

They exist because of an earlier decision. The first review found that thirty-three of thirty-seven figures and ten of thirty tables carried a label no reference ever used, and that every float is placed with [H], so nothing but the reference connects a paragraph to the picture beside it. That was accepted as a must-fix on the grounds that this jury reads figures before prose. Deleting these sentences puts those floats back to being unreferenced.

Both positions are defensible and they point opposite ways. The sentences that interpret rather than announce, such as the one describing three taken atoms and one not-taken atom standing for sixteen instructions, carry content and stay under either policy. It is only the contentless pointers that are in question.

OptionEffect
Keep themEvery figure and table stays reachable from the prose. Twelve low-value sentences remain. This is the status quo and the earlier accepted decision.
Delete themTwelve sentences go. About a dozen figures and several tables return to having no reference in the text, and a reader has nothing telling them which paragraph a picture belongs to.
Delete only the weakestRemove the ones whose figure sits directly under the sentence that already describes it, keep the ones where the float is separated from its paragraph by a page break. Requires checking the built PDF page by page.
